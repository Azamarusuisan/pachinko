# Audio pitch and high-frequency audit — 2026-08-11

## Status

Confirmed analysis covers 13/13 procedural runtime cue families as actual 48 kHz offline PCM, three local reference files, and all 121 sounds generated by the updated `nojiru` audio lab. It does **not** cover 100,000 observations: the manifests contain 489 rows but only 457 unique `(source SHA-256, start, duration)` windows (8 local-reference + 449 generated-reference), leaving 99,543 unique windows uncollected.

The generated comparison baseline is exactly `SHOGO391/nojiru` commit `bbefab094522f17c85342f2fe4822f7e881292eb`. It remains a generated control, not real Japanese-machine calibration.

The three MP3 files under `research-data/local-audio/` are not referenced by runtime code. Their provenance is unverified, so they remain gitignored, `research-only`, and are outside Vite's public/build inputs.

## Measurement method

- Decode as mono 48 kHz PCM. The former 16 kHz conversion was removed because it discarded everything above 8 kHz.
- 2,048-sample Hann FFT, 512-sample hop.
- YIN fundamental estimate from 150–6,000 Hz; accept only confidence >= 0.85.
- Record MIDI/note/cents, pitch coverage, 12-bin chroma, frame timeline, and note histogram.
- Record raw energy ratios: presence 2–4 kHz, brilliance 4–8 kHz, air 8–16 kHz, extreme high 16–20 kHz.
- Record attack time, crest factor, clipping, FFmpeg centroid/rolloff/flux, RMS, peak, and LUFS.
- Render the real `apps/simulator/src/PlayAudio.ts` twice in Chromium `OfflineAudioContext`, not a copied pitch table. Raw browser floats must agree within `2e-6`; per-cue deterministic hashes use signed PCM16 with the low four insignificant bits cleared. The report records that normalization and four-significant-digit display precision.
- Schedule every layer from the same `AudioContext.currentTime`. Runtime cue code no longer uses wall-clock `setTimeout` for delayed notes or noise.

These band ratios are proxies, not standardized DIN 45692 sharpness in acum. A transient/noise cue can be piercing while having no defensible musical note, so pitch and high-frequency energy are kept separate.

### Admission and high-density collision stress

The existing `AudioRegistry` is the runtime admission source: all 13 cues now declare integer priority, cooldown, group, tags, and per-cue maximum concurrency. A global ceiling of 24 active cue voices prevents unbounded mixing; a higher-priority cue can preempt the oldest lowest-priority voice, while equal/lower-priority arrivals are rejected at the ceiling. The production nail policy is priority 10, 12 ms cooldown, and 8 maximum concurrent nail voices.

The offline harness sent 1,000 nail collision calls at the same `AudioContext.currentTime`. Exactly one cue/9 transient nodes were admitted and 999 calls were rejected by the production cooldown; peak voices were 1 of the permitted 8. After rendering, active voices and transient nodes were both 0. Sample peak was 0.008244, clipped-sample ratio was 0, and the 4x Catmull-Rom inter-sample peak proxy was 0.008282. That proxy is deliberately labeled a screening value, not an ITU-R BS.1770 true-peak measurement. The stress PCM hash was `6cbffce626fbbd63f04c6da4d968fef583ae0543446e7825f4cfb5ad94f38771` in two runs; the complete generated report was byte-identical with SHA-256 `bd2f573411d046b3e76eab458d4e64759d698bfdd5bdfc1ceeb509e3a72907b1`.

Separate unit fixtures disable cooldown to exercise the concurrency ceiling directly, fill the global voice ceiling to verify priority preemption, and invoke source `onended` callbacks to prove transient node release. The lottery timeline uses one shared allow-list contract: `hotAlert`/hot confirmation is allowed only for an explicitly hot outcome, and `kyuin`/RUSH confirmation only for a predetermined jackpot. Ordinary and green-reach outcomes cannot invoke either confirmation path; a red miss can invoke the hot alert but not `kyuin` or RUSH.

Nojiru implementation reuse is traceable to the fixed commit above: seeded deterministic context/noise in `packages/audio-lab/src/dsp.ts:18-21,83-88`, semitone-to-Hz conversion in `packages/audio-lab/src/se.ts:34-35`, WAV/manifest generation in `tools/assetgen/src/audio.ts:59-115`, and deterministic/catalog gates in `packages/audio-lab/tests/audio.test.ts:62-71,140-175`. Pachinko applies those patterns to its own runtime `PlayAudio` and records the exact reference revision in both the generated manifest and PCM report.

The added kyuin accent is deliberately short and low-gain: a 52 ms F#8→A8 square layer plus a 34 ms 6.8→10.5 kHz air layer. It adds edge without increasing the existing main cue gains and remains gated to a confirmed jackpot.

## `nojiru` generated-reference results

- 121/121 sources analyzed at 48 kHz: 109 SE + 12 BGM, producing 481 rows and 449 unique fixed windows.
- 93 sources had at least one accepted pitched frame; average pitch coverage was 43.7%.
- Overall average energy ratios: >4 kHz 9.1%, 4–8 kHz 4.7%, 8–16 kHz 3.3%.
- No clipped samples were observed.
- Anticipation cues averaged 27.9% above 4 kHz; confirm cues 7.3%; jackpot cues only 1.3%.
- The top high-band references were all unpitched risers: 58.3–59.7% above 4 kHz and 26.2–26.6% in 8–16 kHz.
- The highest accepted dominant candidate was about 4.802 kHz (D8) in three level-3 confirm cues, but coverage was only 11%; it is a component, not proof of a globally pitched cue.
- Reward level was not monotonic with high-band energy. Level >=4 averaged only 6.2% above 4 kHz. Therefore `nojiru` is useful as a synthesis reference but cannot be treated as calibrated Japanese-machine ground truth.

### BGM tier analysis

The 12 generated BGM controls are three variants each of `calm`, `normal`, `chance`, and `hot`. `npm run audio:music-report` condenses the ignored source, feature, and pitch inputs into the tracked `docs/music-analysis-report.json`; it keeps generated controls separate from real-machine calibration and does not rename a YIN fundamental candidate as a musical key.

| calm | 104 | 9.230771 s | -14.57 | 1,723 Hz | 2,893 Hz | 0.02413 | 0.0795% |
| normal | 122 | 7.868854 s | -13.88 | 1,948 Hz | 3,449 Hz | 0.03090 | 0.1275% |

| hot | 152 | 6.315792 s | -13.14 | 2,470 Hz | 4,839 Hz | 0.04182 | 0.2526% |

All 12 durations agree with the declared four bars / 16 beats within one 48 kHz sample, all hashes are unique, sample clipping is zero, and full-file true peak is -1.31 to -1.30 dBTP. The supplemental full-file `loudnorm` pass gives LRA 0.2–0.8 LU; those standardized values are not injected into the compact report because they are absent from its primary analysis input, so its integrated-LUFS/LRA/dBTP fields remain correctly `null`.

From calm to hot, declared tempo rises 46%, centroid 43%, rolloff 67%, spectral flux 73%, and >4 kHz energy about 3.18x while integrated loudness rises about 1.43 LU. These variables change together, so the corpus demonstrates an authored expectation-tier gradient but cannot identify which variable causes excitement. It is also still low-band-heavy compared with the sharp transient references: even the hot BGM has only 0.253% of measured energy above 4 kHz.

The simulator currently has no BGM asset or BGM playback path, and `story-smooth.mp4` contains video only. This analysis therefore defines a comparison control; it does not claim that music is already integrated or that the generated online-slot loops match a Japanese pachinko machine.

\* Supplemental read-only command: `ffmpeg -i <file> -af loudnorm=I=-23:LRA=7:TP=-2:print_format=json -f null -`.

## External evidence and limits

- [Sleepfreaks pachinko SE recipe](https://sleepfreaks-dtm.com/synth-recipe/pachinko-se/) demonstrates one recreation using a fast attack, rising pitch, detuned unison/distortion, and a short component near F#8 (about 5.92 kHz). It is one recipe, not a manufacturer specification.
- [Dixon et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4225056/) describes slot win sounds as high-pitched and major-mode, but does not isolate pitch as the causal variable.
- [Lee et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7827080/) found greater alarm urgency/arousal for high-frequency and rising signals. Applying alarm findings to pachinko is an implementation hypothesis.
- [Sounds like gambling](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11696015/) shows that gambling environments have identifiable spectral structure; it does not specify the correct pitch for an individual cue.
- dBFS/LUFS cannot determine ear-level dBA without device and volume calibration. Follow [WHO safe listening](https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/deafness-and-hearing-loss-safe-listening) and [NIOSH noise guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/noise/prevent/understand.html); do not create urgency by raising master volume.
